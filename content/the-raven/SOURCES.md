# The Raven — sources and public-domain status

The file `book.json` aligns 18 stanzas. Each stanza is one paragraph id (`s01`–`s18`) across English, Portuguese, and Spanish. Lineation follows the cited edition. Spelling is not modernized.

## English

- Text: Edgar Allan Poe, “The Raven,” in *The Raven and Other Poems*, New York: Wiley and Putnam, 1845, pp. 1–5.
- Transcription: [Wikisource, *The Raven and Other Poems*/The Raven](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Raven_and_Other_Poems/The_Raven), including the 1845 spelling “visiter.”
- Why it is public domain: Poe died on 7 October 1849. The poem was published in 1845. Works published in the United States in 1845 are in the public domain. Countries that use a term of the author’s life plus 70 or 80 years also treat the poem as public domain.

## Portuguese

- Text: Machado de Assis, “O corvo,” in *Poesias completas*, Rio de Janeiro: Livraria Garnier, 1902, pp. 299–305, section Ocidentais. Machado’s translation was first published in 1883.
- Transcription: [Wikisource, O Corvo (tradução de Machado de Assis)](https://pt.wikisource.org/wiki/O_Corvo_(tradu%C3%A7%C3%A3o_de_Machado_de_Assis)), proofread from the Garnier scan (pages 315–321 of that file).
- Why this translation is public domain: the words are Machado’s, not a twentieth-century translator’s. Machado died on 29 September 1908. Brazil (Lei 9.610/98), Portugal, and the European Union protect works for the author’s life plus 70 years, so this translation entered the public domain on 1 January 1979. The 1902 book was published in Machado’s lifetime.
- The reading text keeps two words the 1902 printing has and Wikisource flags with `sic`: “sussura” (for “sussurra”) and “Dize as teus nomes” (for “Dize os teus nomes”).

## Spanish

- Text: Juan Antonio Pérez Bonalde’s translation, first published as *El cuervo*, New York: “La América” Publishing Co. / E. P. Dutton & Co., 1 April 1887. That edition carries a “Copyright, 1887, by E. P. Dutton & Co.” notice. The notice does not still apply: a US work published in 1887 is in the public domain (everything published in the US before 1930 is).
- Reading text: the 1919 Montevideo reprint in *Poemas* (Claudio García, editor), Pérez Bonalde’s translation, validated transcription on [Spanish Wikisource, El cuervo (Pérez Bonalde tr.)](https://es.wikisource.org/wiki/El_cuervo_(P%C3%A9rez_Bonalde_tr.)). A scan of the 1887 book is at the [Biblioteca Nacional de Venezuela](https://bibliotecadigital.bnv.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/El-Cuervo-Edgar-Allan-Poe-Traducci%C3%B3n-por-J-A-Perez-Bonalde-1887.pdf). The 1919 line breaks are kept. The translation itself is Bonalde’s.
- Why this translation is public domain: Pérez Bonalde died in 1892. Spain (life plus 80), Venezuela, Brazil, and the European Union (life plus 70) all treat the translation as public domain. Reprinting it in 1919 did not give the translation a new term.

Fernando Pessoa’s Portuguese and other later Spanish versions are not used. Those translators died more recently, and those texts are not safe to assume are public domain.
