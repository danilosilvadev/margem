# Canon metadata

`shared/canon.ts` is the shelf behind Explore, the book profile, the map, the journey, the family tree, and the opening-lines game. It is not the reading text. A work appears in the reader only when `content/<id>/book.json` exists. Today that is The Raven.

Dates, places, and publication years are the ordinary historical record. Where a birthplace is unknown, the file says so. Homer has no country in the map game for that reason. The Ionian pin is labeled as tradition, not as a documented birthplace.

Influence edges are only relationships a text, a published translation, or a named contemporary document states:

- The Aeneid continues the Troy story of the Iliad and the Odyssey.
- Aristotle’s Poetics (1449a) credits Aeschylus with a second actor and Sophocles with a third.
- In the Comedy, Dante makes Virgil his guide.
- Petrarch’s Latin epic Africa takes the Aeneid as its model. He was crowned in Rome in 1341 for it.
- Boccaccio wrote the Trattatello in laude di Dante in the 1350s.
- Seneca’s tragedies were collected in English in 1581. Titus Andronicus uses that revenge-tragedy form.
- Goethe’s 1815 essay “Shakespeare und kein Ende,” and the Hamlet chapters of Wilhelm Meister.
- The 1818 Frankenstein takes its epigraph from Paradise Lost, Book X.
- Baudelaire’s Poe translations, Histoires extraordinaires, 1856.
- Machado de Assis published a Portuguese Raven in 1883.
- Juan Antonio Pérez Bonalde published El cuervo in New York in 1887. He is on the tree and off the birthplace map.
- In 1846 Belinsky described the author of Poor Folk as a new Gogol.
- Tolstoy wore a medallion portrait of Rousseau as a young man.
- Kafka’s diaries call Der Heizer an imitation of Dickens and name David Copperfield.

No other “influenced by” arrow is drawn. Salon prompts are questions, not posts by invented members. Threads under a salon are signed events from readers.

The birthplace map draws Natural Earth 110m land (public domain) and pins only authors with `onMap` left on. Translators and the extra tree names stay off that plate so Europe can be labeled.

The literary games are explorations. World Map, Journey through Literature, and Family Tree do not ask questions or keep a score. Opening lines in `OPENINGS` remain source notes: each one is the author’s own public-domain wording, or a named public-domain translation (Ormsby 1885, Garnett 1901). The Kafka line is the German first sentence of the 1915 story.

A Russian or German original being public domain does not make a later translation public domain. The file says that on each work.
